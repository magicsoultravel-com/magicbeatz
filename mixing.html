The goal of mixing

Balance every element so each part can be heard clearly and everything works together as one. Mix quietly at first, trust your ears over your eyes, and compare against a reference track you love.

  • 1. Start with a good balance
  • 2. Ears, not eyes
  • 3. Less processing is more
  • 4. Take breaks, stay fresh
  • 5. Reference your mix

Frequency spectrum - what lives where

If two sounds fight, they probably live in the same band. Carve space with EQ instead of turning everything up.

20-60 Hz SUB
Depth, rumble, sub energy.Kick sub, 808s, deep synths - keep this lane almost empty.
60-250 Hz BASS
Punch, warmth, body.Kick, bass, toms, low guitars - the power zone.
250-500 Hz LOW MID
Body and thickness - or boxiness.Snare, guitars, keys, male vocal. Cut here to remove mud.
500 Hz-2 kHz MID
Clarity, definition, focus.Vocals, guitars, keys, snare bite.
2-4 kHz HIGH MID
Presence, attack, intelligibility.Vocals, snare crack, guitar edge, cymbals.
4-6 kHz PRESENCE
Detail, articulation, brilliance.Air on vocals, stick on cymbals, string shine.
6-10 kHz SIZZLE
Sparkle and edge - harshness hides here too.Tame 6-8 kHz if it pierces; keep 8-10 kHz for gloss.
10-20 kHz AIR
Openness, breath, shimmer.Cymbals, hats, vocal breath. A little goes a long way.

Quick EQ tips

Cut to clean, boost to shine. Small moves: cuts 2-4 dB, boosts 1-3 dB.

High-pass filter

Roll off lows on everything except kick + bass. Start 20-30 Hz, raise until it thins, then back off.

Fix low-end clash

Kick vs bass fighting? Cut 200-300 Hz on one of them so the other owns the lows.

Reduce mud (250-500 Hz)

Boomy or boxy? Dip 2-3 dB with a wide bell. Instant clarity on vocals and guitars.

Presence boost (2-5 kHz)

Help parts cut through. Narrow +1-2 dB lift for vocal intelligibility and snare snap.

Tame harshness (6-8 kHz)

Piercing hats or sibilance? Gentle 2-3 dB dip. De-ess vocals around 6 kHz.

Air and sparkle (10 kHz+)

High shelf +1-2 dB for openness on masters, overheads and breathy vocals.

Compression breakdown

A compressor shrinks dynamic range: loud parts get quieter, then makeup gain lifts everything.

Threshold
The level where compression kicks in.
Ratio
How hard it squeezes (3:1 gentle, 8:1+ firm).
Attack
How fast it grabs (slow keeps punch, fast tames peaks).
Release
How fast it lets go (too fast pumps, too slow dulls).
Makeup
Gain to restore level lost to compression.

Starting points

threshold -18 dBratio 3:1-4:1attack 10-30 msrelease 50-200 msmakeup +3-6 dB

Vocals: faster attack, medium release. Drums: slower attack to keep snap. Bass: medium attack, auto release.

Volume and gain staging

Headroom is your friend. Loud comes last, at mastering.

-12 to -6 dBFS
Track input peaks. Plenty of room, clean converters.
Faders = balance
Fix tone with EQ, dynamics with compression - not the fader.
-6 dBFS on mix bus
Keep peaks under this so the master chain can breathe.
0 dB unity
No boost or cut. Start here, then carve.

Quick effects guide

Send, do not insert (except saturation + limiter). Less is more.

Reverb
Space + depth. Short room for drums, hall for vocals. High-pass it at ~200 Hz.
Delay
Repeats + groove. Slapback 80-140 ms for thickness, 1/8 dotted for width.
Chorus
Width + movement. Subtle on guitars and keys, skip on kick and bass.
Saturation
Warmth + character. A touch on bass, vocals, mix bus glues everything.
Limiter
Safety net on the master. Catch 1-2 dB of peaks only - mixing is not mastering.

Pro mixing checklist

Run this before you bounce. If every box ticks, you are done.

  • Gain staged: peaks around -12 to -6 dBFS, bus under -6 dBFS
  • Unnecessary lows rolled off every non-bass track
  • Every element owns its frequency and pan space
  • Dynamics controlled - no jumps, no pumping
  • EQ cuts are clean, boosts are small and sweet
  • Reverb and delay sit behind the dry sound, not on top
  • Levels balanced at low volume and on small speakers
  • Checked on two systems + one reference track

Tip: export, take a 10-minute break, listen once on earbuds. Fix one thing, not ten.
